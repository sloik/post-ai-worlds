# AI 2040: studying a recommendation without mistaking it for a forecast

Detailed scenarios can reveal connections that a list of trends misses. They can also feel more certain than their premises justify. A useful reading separates what the authors recommend, what they expect conditional on the recommendation, and what they think will actually happen.

## What the source says

The AI Futures Project presents *AI 2040: Plan A* as a positive policy scenario and primarily a recommendation rather than its best prediction of the future. Its public introduction describes coordinated slowing, greater research transparency and a wider distribution of frontier capability. Those are the authors' proposals, not institutions this chapter claims already exist. [Original scenario](https://ai-2040.com/); [authors' announcement, July 2026](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/pFzctpJBat95SrCyC/ai-2040-plan-a).

This distinction changes how to evaluate the text. Failure to implement a recommendation is not necessarily failure of every conditional mechanism it describes. Conversely, an appealing destination does not establish that the proposed policy path is feasible, stable or desirable for every affected group.

## Questions to carry across

Our resource-and-institutions perspective asks how the proposed arrangement is financed, verified and enforced. Which actors bear the cost of compliance? What can an outside country observe? How would a violation be distinguished from ordinary technical progress? What changes if a participant withdraws?

The scenario also highlights a question that cannot be reduced to model alignment: who controls powerful systems after technical problems are solved? A distribution of nominally separate companies may still depend on common infrastructure. A transparent arrangement can still leave some communities unable to influence its objectives. These are challenges to investigate, not reasons to dismiss coordination.

## What not to import

This chapter does not adopt the source's arrival dates or quantitative judgments. Its recommendations should not become hidden premises in the rest of the project. Contributors proposing an alternative should name the changed assumptions and explain how their own arrangement handles evasion, concentration and practical oversight.

Plan A's letter belongs to that external project's namespace. It is not ALIGN-Directed, ERA-A or OMNI-001. A conceptual link to OMNI-103 can help discuss public capacity, but does not make the scenarios equivalent.

No forecast registration, endorsement of every proposal or executable assurance follows from this comparison.
