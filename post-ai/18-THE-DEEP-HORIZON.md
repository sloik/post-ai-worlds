# The deep horizon: physics, institutions and long-lived consequences

Long horizons invite impressive narratives and weak accountability. Beyond a few decades, a precise calendar can create confidence without evidence. We instead distinguish physical constraints, engineering possibilities, institutional assumptions and deliberate fiction. A limit is not a schedule; a possible design is not an expected civilisation.

## Constraints with different meanings

Computation and physical work involve energy and heat. In space, thermal design includes radiating heat to the external environment; moving hardware off Earth does not remove the need to manage that heat. NASA's spacecraft guidance describes practical thermal-control considerations, not a complete economic case for orbital computing. [NASA thermal-control reference](https://www.nasa.gov/smallsat-institute/sst-soa/thermal-control/).

Information processing also has thermodynamic questions. Experimental research tests Landauer's principle for logically irreversible erasure. It should not be misread as the energy cost of every useful AI operation or a prediction of achievable system efficiency. [Bérut and colleagues, 2012](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature10872).

Communication across distance imposes latency. More resources do not automatically permit a geographically larger system to coordinate at the speed of a tightly integrated smaller one. What that means for an organisation depends on which decisions require coordination and which can remain local.

## Expansion does not settle government

Suppose much larger computational and material resources become available. Communities would still need to decide who may use them, how conflicts are addressed and how commitments survive change. A long-lived system might preserve records while altering the interpretation of its founding purpose. Continuity of data is not automatically continuity of authority or values.

Distributed settlements or machines could widen practical exit while making enforcement across distances harder. Conversely, a common technical dependency could concentrate power even where physical space is plentiful. Neither centralisation nor decentralisation should be assumed merely from scale.

## What present research can usefully do

Study recoverability, reliable local autonomy, resource accounting and institutional revision now. Those questions remain relevant if extreme scenarios never occur. A far-future hypothesis earns its place when it exposes a current design choice or a neglected assumption, rather than only enlarging the imagined machinery.

OMNI abundance and adaptive-collective universes are conceptual comparisons, not extrapolated probabilities. This chapter makes no century-scale forecast, investment thesis or executable-assurance claim.
