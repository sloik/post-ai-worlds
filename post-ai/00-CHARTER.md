# Charter: studying an AI-transformed world

The question is broader than which model will be strongest: **what institutions, resources and relationships would an AI-transformed society need, and who would control them?** This track explores that question alongside the organisational designs in the companion track. It is a research programme, not a prediction that a single future must arrive.

## The premise we can vary

Some chapters assume that useful cognitive work becomes much cheaper. Others examine disappointing gains, difficult deployment or concentrated access. Cheap reasoning, dependable agency, affordable robotics and broad material abundance are different premises. None should enter an argument silently through the word “AI.”

There is a historical reason to examine organisation as well as technical performance. Research on the productivity paradox identifies implementation lags and complementary changes as possible explanations for a gap between promising technology and measured economic results. That motivates asking what must change around AI; it does not provide a timetable or guarantee a productivity revolution. [Brynjolfsson, Rock and Syverson, 2017](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24001).

## Method

We keep observations, assumptions, mechanisms, normative proposals and forecasts separate. An observation needs a source and scope. A mechanism needs an account of how one state could produce another. A normative proposal needs reasons that people can contest. A forecast needs a future event, resolution conditions and a declared judgment before the outcome is known.

The project welcomes contradictory scenarios. One branch can have affordable independent AI while essential infrastructure remains concentrated. Another can have abundant output without meaningful public control. Coherence means stating those combinations clearly, rather than making the optimistic branch favourable in every dimension.

## What contributors can improve

Useful contributions identify a weak assumption, supply better evidence, expose a missing affected group or show a practical institutional alternative. A numerical claim should carry its date and denominator. A successful demonstration should explain what it leaves untested. A compelling story should identify the observation that would change it.

The numbered OMNI universes are shared conceptual reference points. Mapping a chapter to one does not establish that its assumptions are true or that its prose has been formally checked. This public edition begins with its own evidence boundary; it makes no claim of executable assurance for these chapters.
