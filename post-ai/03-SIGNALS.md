# Signals: deciding what would change our minds

A signal is useful when it distinguishes explanations. Another impressive demonstration may confirm that a task can be performed while saying little about affordability, deployment, social benefit or control. A research dashboard should record that distinction rather than accumulate encouraging headlines.

## Candidate instruments

| Question | Evidence worth seeking | What it would not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Are practical task costs falling? | Reproducible outcomes including human review, serving, maintenance and errors | General autonomy or cheaper physical production |
| Is infrastructure limiting deployment? | Dated service, connection or capacity records in a specified location | Permanent scarcity or durable supplier profits |
| Are adopters benefiting? | Independently assessed outcomes and costs with a defensible comparison | That every sector benefits or AI caused every change |
| Can users leave? | Actual successful migration with continuity and usable records | Effective exit for everyone |
| Can public institutions exercise control? | An audit, appeal or supplier replacement that changes an outcome | General democratic legitimacy |
| Can citizens build alternatives? | Sustained useful output with accountable governance and disclosed dependencies | Frontier parity or complete self-sufficiency |
| Is software exposure shrinking? | Dated time from AI-assisted disclosure to deployed fix in widely used software, with discovery volume and patch quality | Attacker uptake, net defender advantage or that unpatched systems are rare |
| Is AI speeding up AI research? | Repeated estimates by an unaffiliated assessor using a fixed method, with the underlying evidence open to inspection | Progress on tasks that cannot be checked, a continuing trend or effects outside the laboratory |
| Are relationships changing? | Longitudinal evidence distinguishing supplementation from displacement | A causal effect inferred from adoption alone |

The exposure row follows OpenAI's November 2026 Linux kernel report. It says that during its ten-day scan and analysis, maintainers had already patched 61 of the reported privilege-escalation flaws upstream and staged patches for 43 more, while 299 were in public discussion. These producer-reported counts show maintainers moving quickly, but they are not a measure of deployed exposure. [OpenAI, 2026](https://openai.com/index/a-thousand-fewer-paths-to-root/).

The research-speed row follows two 2026 reports. METR's assessment estimates that Anthropic is improving its models on well-specified tasks at about twice its earlier rate, and says 1.3x or 3x would not surprise it. [METR, 2026](https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-05-anthropic-ai-rd-acceleration-assessment/). Anthropic's prototype index reports that its model "leads" 26% of the company's AI research and development work as of August 2026, up from under 1% in February, with none of it fully autonomous. One of the company's own models assigns the levels over a task basket frozen in July 2026. [Anthropic, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/institute/measuring-pace-of-ai-development). A November 2026 essay reads these as the start of recursive self-improvement and forecasts automated AI research by 2028. [Patel, 2026](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/rsi). That is one interpretation. The reports cover one laboratory, rest partly on redacted or self-judged evidence, and measure checkable tasks. A second assessment using the same method would distinguish a continuing acceleration from a single step.

These are candidates. No baseline search, numerical threshold or resolved outcome is asserted by listing them. A future investigator must define the population, interval, comparison and qualifying evidence before scoring a result.

## Preserve three dates

Record when the underlying event occurred, when its source was published and when we inspected it. An old event encountered today is not a new observation of progress. A revised source should retain the earlier interpretation so that changed beliefs remain auditable.

## Separate monitoring from forecasting

A monitoring question asks what is happening. A forecast additionally commits to a future event, deadline and uncertainty judgment. This public chapter does not import historical forecasts or retroactively register predictions. Any new forecast should first establish that its event has not already occurred and explain how missing evidence will be treated.

Measurement practice offers a useful analogy: traceability and uncertainty belong with a result rather than being optional decorations. The analogy does not turn a social indicator into a physical calibration. [NIST metrological traceability guidance](https://www.nist.gov/metrology/metrological-traceability).

Each observation can inform a named OMNI premise without confirming a whole universe. Missing public evidence is not automatically evidence of absence. This chapter proposes manual research instruments, not an operating monitor or a formal proof.
