# Public scenarios and resource economics

Capability forecasts and resource analysis answer different questions. A scenario can describe rapid progress in AI research while leaving construction, adoption and institutional change comparatively implicit. Resource analysis can expose those requirements while underestimating how much a new capability changes the available engineering choices. Reading the two together is more useful than treating either as complete.

## A public argument to examine

Leopold Aschenbrenner's June 2024 *Situational Awareness* argues for rapid capability progress, expanding compute and an increasingly consequential strategic competition. Those are the author's prospective claims, not outcomes this chapter certifies. The public essays provide an inspectable argument whose premises can be challenged separately. [Original essay collection](https://situational-awareness.ai/).

Our question is what must connect a technical advance to an outcome outside the laboratory. A design needs materials and manufacturing. A useful service needs a buyer and a workable organisation. A public deployment needs an appropriate authority and a way to address harm. These connections may accelerate, but they cannot be omitted from the explanation.

## Avoid two opposite mistakes

The first mistake is extrapolating a benchmark improvement directly into economic transformation. Research on implementation lags offers reasons to expect organisational work between capability and measured productivity. It does not establish a fixed delay for AI or show that every incumbent must lose. [Brynjolfsson, Rock and Syverson](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24001).

The second mistake is declaring today's physical bottleneck permanent. Better tools can change designs, substitute inputs or improve throughput. To examine such a claim, identify the proposed intervention, remaining dependencies and evidence of a working response. A statement that intelligence will solve the problem is incomplete until those links are supplied.

## Compare explicit cases

Consider a cheap software service, a regulated clinical product and a new industrial facility. They may use similar reasoning capabilities while facing different verification, financing and deployment requirements. Their timelines should not be inferred from one another.

This chapter is a public methodological comparison, not a stock-selection framework or a recommendation to hold particular assets. Its contribution is an invitation to connect assumptions: what capability, what resources, what institution, and what observable result? A counterexample can improve that chain without deciding the whole future of AI.
