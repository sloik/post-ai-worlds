# Glossary

These terms are analytical tools used in this project. A definition does not establish that its mechanism dominates the real world. Where a word has a technical or legal meaning elsewhere, the chapter's stated scope governs this project's use.

**Abundance.** Adequate provision of a specified basket within a declared setting. It does not mean infinite resources, universal access or the disappearance of politics.

**Accountability.** An arrangement for identifying decisions, demanding explanations and corrections, and applying appropriate consequences. A name in a log does not establish that all these functions work.

**Alignment.** A relationship between system behaviour and intended objectives. Relevant questions include whose objectives, which conditions and what happens when oversight changes.

**Assumption.** A premise used to explore a consequence. Some assumptions are empirical, some hypothetical and some normative. Their roles should remain explicit.

**Binding constraint.** A factor limiting a specified outcome at a particular time. Its existence does not by itself establish who captures economic value.

**Capability.** Ability to perform a stated class of tasks under specified conditions and resources. A general adjective such as “intelligent” is not a task specification.

**Conceptual relevance.** A connection worth discussing between a chapter and a universe. It is weaker than asserting the chapter's premises or admitting its conclusions in that universe.

**Contestability.** Practical ability to question a consequential decision, obtain independent scrutiny and seek a meaningful change or remedy.

**Continuity.** Preservation of the records, state or commitments needed to continue a process. Different forms of continuity need different tests; none automatically demonstrates subjective identity.

**Demand.** In this track, the combination of needs, willingness and ability to pay, and institutions that connect those to provision. Need alone is not a completed transaction.

**Empirical support.** Observations bearing on a claim. Support is scoped to the study or measurement and should include contrary evidence and uncertainty.

**Exit.** Moving to another arrangement while retaining whatever rights and resources make that move practical. Nominal permission differs from a usable alternative.

**Forecast.** A prospective judgment about a defined future event, with timing and resolution rules. A scenario or a monitoring question is not automatically a forecast.

**Formal assurance.** A result about specified properties of a precise model or implementation under stated assumptions. It is not empirical confirmation of a social theory. No vision chapter currently claims it.

**Granted provision.** A service or benefit dependent on another party's continuing discretion. This shorthand does not replace examination of actual contracts, institutions or remedies.

**Institutional capacity.** Ability to make, implement, scrutinise and revise consequential collective decisions. It differs from nominal authority and from computational power.

**Legitimacy.** A justified basis for exercising authority, including how affected people are represented and protected. Efficiency, popularity and customer retention do not settle it alone.

**Moral patient.** An entity whose possible interests may deserve protection. This does not itself establish liability, legal personhood or public mandate.

**OMNI universe.** A numbered, scoped bundle of assumptions used across the project. An identifier is neither a probability nor a proof certificate.

**Scenario.** An account of what could follow under declared assumptions. Useful scenarios expose mechanisms and alternatives rather than merely naming attractive or frightening outcomes.

**Supplementation and substitution.** A technology can add support to an existing activity or relationship, or replace some of it. These mechanisms can coexist and require separate evidence.

**Verification.** Checking a stated claim against relevant criteria and evidence. It matters whether the criteria themselves describe the intended outcome and whether the verifier is independent in the relevant way.
