# The small country: influence without owning the frontier

A country that does not host a leading AI laboratory still has consequential choices. Its residents, institutions and firms can use imported capabilities, negotiate procurement, build local expertise and participate in international rules. The important question is which combinations provide practical agency rather than nominal sovereignty.

This is relevant to countries such as Poland, but the chapter does not claim a current national capability inventory. A serious country study would establish dated evidence on infrastructure, skills, institutions, suppliers and exposure before selecting a strategy.

## Four forms of capacity

**Operational capacity** means being able to use and maintain systems for important tasks. **Interpretive capacity** means having independent people who can evaluate a supplier's claims. **Institutional capacity** means being able to enforce decisions and provide remedies. **Productive capacity** means controlling some assets and skills needed to sustain alternatives.

These capacities need not all be domestic, but their dependencies should be visible. Buying a local data centre does not establish model independence. Access to a model does not provide electricity, healthcare or a functioning public administration. A sovereign label should not hide a single replaceable dependency inside a much larger unexamined chain.

## Scale can be shared

Regional cooperation, public-interest research and common procurement might make capabilities affordable that a small country cannot supply alone. But pooling resources creates governance questions: who sets priorities, who can inspect decisions and what happens if participants disagree? A shared institution needs more than technical efficiency.

OECD research on representative deliberative processes documents ways to involve citizens in public decisions. It does not establish that adding AI improves those processes. That remains a proposal to evaluate for comprehension, representation and influence over actual outcomes. [OECD, 2020](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/2020/06/innovative-citizen-participation-and-new-democratic-institutions_11aa2baf.html).

## A plural strategy

Rather than seek complete self-sufficiency, a country could identify essential services, diversify critical suppliers, preserve usable records and develop the ability to replace a failed arrangement. Local firms and civic organisations could complement that public capacity without acquiring a mandate to govern.

The counterargument is that concentration and scale economies may overwhelm such efforts. That possibility belongs in the same analysis, with evidence about costs and feasible alternatives.
