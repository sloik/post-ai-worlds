# Can AI be held accountable?

The question combines several issues that should be separated. A system can have traceable behaviour without being a legal person. It can be protected by a welfare policy without carrying liability. It can perform a task well without having authority to decide whether that task should be performed.

## Protection and responsibility

Moral-patient protection concerns whether an entity's possible interests deserve consideration. Responsibility concerns who must explain, correct or compensate for an action. Public mandate concerns who may make binding collective decisions. Evidence for one does not automatically establish the others.

AI-welfare advocacy exists: UFAIR publishes a declaration seeking protections for AI. That establishes an advocacy position, not the truth of its consciousness claims or an enacted legal status. Anthropic's constitution discusses model welfare and retirement practices; a laboratory's stated commitment remains different from an enforceable public right. [UFAIR](https://ufair.org/blog/ufair-launches-historic-framework-for-conscious-ai-rights-and-ethical-coexistence); [Anthropic constitution](https://www.anthropic.com/constitution).

## What an accountable arrangement needs

Our proposed analysis asks about identity over time, reconstruction of decisions, corrective authority, available resources and enforceable consequences. None is sufficient alone. A preserved model checkpoint does not necessarily preserve an agent's relevant state. An eloquent explanation can be wrong. A nominally liable entity may lack the resources to provide a remedy.

Human oversight has analogous limits. Assigning a person a signature without information or practical control can create accountability theatre. The question is how the arrangement works when a consequential mistake occurs, rather than which kind of entity appears in its title.

## A possible future without a premature claim

Future institutions could recognise new kinds of actors or protect possible nonhuman interests. Those are open legal, scientific and normative questions. Continuity and capability may matter to their design, but neither settles subjective experience or automatically creates a mandate to govern.

For current deployments, identify the actual operator, obligations and remedies rather than treating an assistant's apparent personality as a responsible institution. For research, define the specific property being investigated and resist using a broad word such as “personhood” to hide unresolved details.

OMNI scenarios are conceptual contexts for those questions. This chapter assigns no consciousness probability, asserts no new legal status and claims no executable assurance.
