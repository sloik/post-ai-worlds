# What the objections to recursive self-improvement change

**Reviewed 25 November 2026.** In the 24 November follow-up to his 13 November essay, Dwarkesh Patel and Max Farrens distinguish AI assistance already visible in labs from the harder claim that a whole research loop can run without essential human work. Patel describes 2028 as his median year for matching the best AI researchers, gives a rough one-in-four chance of superintelligence in 2027, and describes 2028–29 as his expected period for a much faster transition. These are his judgments, with no operational resolution criteria in the discussion. His end-2027 prediction of agents that own open-ended projects is a separate, earlier claim. [Publisher transcript](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/rsiobjections) · [Original essay](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/rsi) · [Video](https://youtu.be/Em6tbhj9o6A)

## What to track

- **Omnicorp.** For recurring work, compare accepted improvements per week and per compute budget with the existing workflow. Track elapsed time in active work, compute queues, human decisions, verification and rework. Measure how much help a new agent needs on the next unfamiliar task after a week or month. Keep the evaluation and records outside the improving agent’s authority, and preserve approval and rollback.
- **Post-AI.** Compare internal and public capability where audits permit. Track actual compute allocation and access, completed projects, unaided oversight capacity and independent incident review. Assess whether these changes precede broad deployment, shifts in paid hours or changes in who owns productive capacity. A reliable persistent agent and an audited improvement loop are distinct milestones.
- **On 5 February 2027**, look for fixed-budget checkpoint replication, an unaffiliated research-rate reassessment, an updated AI-led index, evidence of durable project ownership and published evidence about the next experiment bottleneck. Project ownership is a candidate indicator. Adjudication would require task scope, sustained observation, a quality floor, intervention limits, deployment proof and missing-data rules.
