## Post-AI and Omnicorp implications

**Post-AI.** Watch design time, operator-approved process changes, and robot operations as distinct mechanisms. A foundry may ship sooner if trials, rework or queueing are the bottleneck. Measure accepted units and order-to-delivery time before inferring capacity gains. Utilisation, operator interventions, downtime and total cost matter in robotics. Paid hours, wages, prices and control over productive assets still determine how plant-level gains reach workers and households. That is the practical distinction between the material capacity and ownership assumptions in OMNI-201 and OMNI-202.

**Omnicorp.** Prometheus illustrates a possible operating model: share engineering tools with incumbents, learn from physical feedback and potentially participate in the upside. For a small organisation, the immediate question is whether one existing operator values a measurable improvement. On an authorised workflow, start with a fixed acceptance metric and historical baseline, then compare offline recommendations with operator decisions and measured results. Keep process logs, inputs and acceptance data under the operator's control, with agreed rights over derivative data and the ability to export records. Compare the existing manual or conventional automation baseline before investing in specialised models or robot hardware. Financing and deployment would require their own decisions.

## Proposed forecast on sustained casting scrap reduction

**Draft, 40% subjective probability, Argo, information cutoff 27 November 2026.** Will at least two metals foundry operators under different ultimate control publicly document at least a **20% relative reduction in casting scrap sustained over 90 consecutive calendar days** on a named production line and identified casting pattern or part family, following a machine-learning recommendation adopted in regular production, between 28 November 2026 and 31 December 2029 (UTC)?

- **Qualifying evidence.** Require two cases with the post-change measurement windows and first public results inside the forecast window. Each case must name the operator, site and line (a stable plant-specific line label suffices), specify the AI-influenced process change, and give dates plus counts of scrapped and produced units for both the 90-day post-change window and the immediately preceding 90-day baseline. Use the same part scope and scrap definition, a nonzero baseline rate, and disclose material changes in mix, inspection and concurrent interventions. A suitable matched comparison may account for a concurrent change. An operator's detailed report or an unaffiliated published audit may qualify. A vendor claim alone, a pilot outside regular production, a simulation, inspection-only automation or unreported denominators cannot qualify. Human approval of a recommended change is allowed. Count each operator once.
- **Outside view and opposing cases.** Condals demonstrates larger reported reductions on two patterns at one operator, with selected commissioning results and little public baseline detail. The GF and BMW Landshut work show independent operational settings for plant ML, with too little comparable detail to establish the proposed event. Audi adds evidence of adjacent deployment, rather than another foundry result. Search covered public foundry case studies, company reports and published engineering work. There is no denominator of deployed interventions and disclosed results. Forty percent is a rounded judgment for two *publicly auditable* cases over three years. Demand, instrumentation and an existing supplier market favour the event. Changing inputs, concurrent engineering, undisclosed scrap counts and early success reporting weigh against it. [BMW Landshut research](https://epub.uni-regensburg.de/41301/1/thesis.pdf).
- **Perspective and update plan.** A plant manager may care about economic savings without releasing counts. The forecast must account for that disclosure barrier even if implementation advances. There is one forecaster and no pooled estimate. Recheck on 5 February 2027 and annually thereafter if registered. Raise on the first qualifying case or named multi-operator programmes with published measurement plans. Lower if reporting stays at initial percentages without counts or if controlled comparisons show gains disappear over time.
- **Resolution and scoring.** Review public records through 28 February 2030 to locate and check eligible results first published by 31 December 2029. Resolve YES for two qualifying operators and NO for fewer, including missing or confidential results. Record source URLs and calculations. If accepted before outcomes, score the accepted initial probability with binary Brier loss; log later probabilities separately with dates and rationale. The Observatory's issued forecasts do not include this proposal.
