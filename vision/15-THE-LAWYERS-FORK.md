# The lawyer's fork: provision that can be withheld

Imagine two people receiving the same useful service. One has a durable claim, workable remedies and alternatives. The other depends on a provider's continuing discretion. Their present consumption may look identical while their future bargaining positions differ sharply.

We call this the owned-versus-granted question. The language is an analytical shorthand, not a legal classification. Ownership can be incomplete, unenforceable or tied to resources a person cannot maintain. A granted service can be dependable under strong public institutions. Actual arrangements need closer description than either label provides.

## What practical control would require

Ask which decisions a person can make without discretionary approval, which assets or records they can retain, and what happens after refusal or disagreement. Then ask whether the promised remedy can be exercised. A written entitlement that nobody can enforce may protect less than a technically replaceable service supplied by several credible providers.

The converse matters too: downloading a model is not complete independence. It may still require expensive hardware, continuing maintenance, compatible data and access to physical services. A claim about sovereignty should name those dependencies instead of stopping at software possession.

## How the fork could develop

In OMNI-201, plural provision might make alternatives affordable and allow communities to bargain. In OMNI-202, material abundance might coexist with concentrated control. In OMNI-401, disappointing gains could leave familiar scarcity and bargaining structures largely intact. None of these scenarios follows just from current product quality.

Concentration in AI infrastructure is a relevant observable condition. The OECD's analysis describes structural competition concerns; it does not show that all dependence is coercive or all ownership arrangements succeed. [OECD, 2025](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/competition-in-artificial-intelligence-infrastructure_623d1874-en.html).

## An institutional rather than individual answer

The useful response need not be a household attempting to reproduce every essential service. Cooperatives, public capacity, professional associations and independent suppliers can distribute the cost of alternatives. Such institutions also need accountable governance; a cooperative label does not settle who actually decides.

The normative aim is to preserve people's ability to challenge consequential decisions and build another arrangement when necessary. The empirical task is to determine which structures make that possible in a specified setting.

This chapter establishes no legal entitlement, inevitable political trajectory or executable assurance.
