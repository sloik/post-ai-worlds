# The exception that will not hold: capability and standing

Debates about AI often protect a human role by naming a task machines supposedly cannot perform. Such claims are empirical and can be overturned by new evidence. That does not mean every human interest disappears when a task becomes automatable. It means competence is the wrong foundation for some kinds of standing.

## Different questions need different tests

Whether a system can produce a correct answer is a capability question. Whether someone is entitled to participate in a decision is a normative and institutional question. Whether a named operator can compensate an injured person concerns responsibility and enforceability. A performance ranking cannot resolve all three.

Consider an automated service that becomes better at selecting a treatment or allocating a resource. Better outcomes would matter. People would still have reasons to ask who set the objective, how exceptional circumstances are handled and how an error can be challenged. Demanding those arrangements need not deny the performance improvement.

## Avoid a new permanent exception

It would repeat the same mistake to declare that responsibility must belong to humans forever because present AI systems cannot bear it in a familiar way. Future institutional arrangements are open questions. But continuity, legal standing, resources and effective consequence-bearing would have to be demonstrated; fluent explanations alone would not supply them.

The project therefore treats accountable operation as a requirement to define, not a label automatically earned by a model or a person. A human signature can be nominal too. The useful questions concern actual judgment, available remedies and who remains answerable when the consequences arrive.

## Dignity is not a productivity prize

Our normative starting point is that people's standing should not depend on outperforming machines. The Universal Declaration of Human Rights provides a public expression of equal dignity and rights. It does not establish a solution to every question about possible nonhuman moral status. [United Nations](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights/).

This distinction allows two positions to coexist: technical performance can improve dramatically, and public authority still needs justification. Neither requires inventing an eternally unautomatable task.

OMNI-001 concerns present accountable operation; other universes explore changed capabilities and institutions. These are conceptual links. No future legal status, consciousness conclusion or executable proof is asserted.
