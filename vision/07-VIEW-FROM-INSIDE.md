# The inside view: continuity, context and accountable assistants

A long-running assistant can accumulate records of decisions, preferences, unfinished work and corrections. Those records may become useful even when the underlying model changes. This suggests a distinction between **replaceable computation** and **the continuity a person or organisation wants to preserve**.

The argument does not depend on an assistant having consciousness or a persistent inner self. It concerns inspectable information and behaviour: whether a new system can reconstruct the relevant history, respect current permissions and continue useful work without repeating avoidable mistakes.

## Maintenance is part of the cost

A useful record is not merely stored. It must be found, interpreted and corrected when circumstances change. Contradictory instructions, obsolete assumptions and missing context can make a large archive less useful than a small, well-maintained one. A claim that memory compounds value should therefore include maintenance effort and the consequences of error.

One proposed measurement compares the total cost of continuing a task with and without retained context, under a fixed outcome test. Another compares migration between providers, including failed reconstructions and required human repair. These are experimental proposals, not reported measurements.

## Portability is more than an export button

An exported conversation history may omit tool state, permissions, attachments or the meanings assigned to earlier decisions. Practical portability requires a recipient that can use the information and a person able to inspect what was transferred. Keeping a copy can improve options without establishing independence from infrastructure, licenses or compatible software.

## The assistant's authority remains a separate record

An assistant may know how a task was performed without having authority to repeat it. Familiarity with a person does not grant consent to publish their records, spend money or speak on their behalf. A continuity design should preserve those distinctions instead of treating old access as permanent permission.

NIST's AI risk framework provides a public reference for considering systems in their contexts of use and managing risks throughout their lifecycle. Our continuity and migration proposals are specific design questions, not NIST-certified capabilities. [NIST AI RMF](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework).

OMNI-001 and OMNI-301 are relevant conceptual settings for retained organisational context. This public essay reports no household operations, private measurements or successful recovery experiment. It has no executable assurance.
