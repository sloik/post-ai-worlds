# The vision: abundance changes the scarce things

Suppose AI makes a useful class of cognitive tasks much cheaper. The immediate consequence is not that everything becomes free. More proposals can compete for the same electricity connection, construction crew, clinical study, customer's attention or permission to operate. The important question becomes **which constraints move, which persist, and who can change them**.

## Three horizons, without a clock

We use ERA-A for expanding computational and organisational infrastructure, ERA-B for wider deployment and adjustment, and ERA-C for possible broad automation and institutional redesign. These are analytical horizons, not dated predictions or a required sequence. A country can occupy several at once: advanced software alongside constrained housing, medicine and infrastructure.

Energy illustrates the connection between cognition and physical systems. The IEA's 2025 analysis treats data centres and electricity supply as an interconnected planning problem. The relevant quantities include location and the timing of deliverable power, not just annual national generation. This supports studying infrastructure; it does not establish that every supplier captures lasting economic value. [IEA, *Energy and AI*, 2025](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai).

## Constraints can be targets

A shortage can invite investment, substitution or redesign. Better intelligence might accelerate those responses, but building and qualifying new capacity still requires resources and elapsed time. An asset can be physically important while becoming commercially unattractive if competitors expand, customers integrate production or contracts allocate the gains elsewhere.

We therefore distinguish a binding constraint from a durable entitlement to its returns. The same caution applies to knowledge: a better model may lower task costs while shifting bargaining power towards whoever owns distribution, data or infrastructure.

## The human and political remainder

Consent, representation and responsibility do not disappear when production improves. Someone must decide which risks are acceptable, who receives benefits and how failures can be challenged. Those are institutional questions rather than residual technical defects.

A useful world must offer more than plentiful output. People need practical alternatives, relationships, participation and ways to influence consequential decisions. Material abundance with concentrated control is a distinct scenario from abundance with plural ownership. OMNI-201 and OMNI-202 make that difference legible; neither is assigned a probability here.

The test of this vision is whether it helps discriminate between developments. If it explains every outcome after the fact, it explains too little. The [signal chapter](03-SIGNALS.md) proposes a disciplined way to record what would support, challenge or narrow the argument.

Universe references are conceptual; this chapter has no executable assurance.
