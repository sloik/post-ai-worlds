# Governance

This is an open research project maintained by the repository owner, **sloik**, and any maintainers the owner explicitly appoints. Contributions are proposals until reviewed and merged. Public participation does not transfer operational authority over an organisation or confer a mandate to govern anyone.

## Decisions and review

Maintainers decide repository scope, editing standards, releases and merges. Contributors can challenge factual claims, assumptions, conclusions, methods and governance through issues or discussions. Small corrections can be reviewed directly; changes to shared world assumptions, forecast criteria or assurance boundaries need an explicit explanation and review of affected records.

Maintain one shared numbered register. Preserve stable identities and the reason for revisions or forks. Do not silently replace a historical prediction with a better question or copy assurance between changed inputs.

Where practical, someone other than the author reviews substantive changes. A reviewer checks the source-to-claim correspondence and the intended meaning of a formal statement, as well as whether a tool ran successfully. Mechanical checks support review; they do not replace judgment or grant empirical truth.

## Disagreement is useful

State which kind of disagreement is involved: observation, empirical premise, logical inference, model encoding, value or project decision. Record plausible alternatives as explicit scoped branches when that helps a real question. Do not multiply universes merely to avoid a clear contradiction.

Maintainers can accept, request changes, defer or decline a proposal. Explain consequential decisions in the issue or pull request. Declining a contribution does not make its argument false. An unresolved disagreement may remain visible with its evidence and scope.

Reactions and polls can inform a discussion; they do not establish scientific validity or replace maintainer review. Generated participation is not additional public representation, independent evidence or extra voting weight.

## Authority boundaries

Exploring a hypothetical world does not suspend current responsibilities or authorise acting it out. Repository content and contribution activity do not automatically authorise expenditure, external messages, deployment, collection of personal data or autonomous agents.

Values and design commitments remain labelled as such. A formal proof cannot establish democratic legitimacy, and service quality does not itself confer a public mandate. See the [world register](worlds/README.md) and [assurance boundaries](formal/README.md).

## Changes to governance

Propose governance changes through a pull request, with the reason and consequences stated. The repository owner retains the final merge decision. Licensing changes require an explicit decision and must respect contributors' existing rights; a new policy does not silently relicense prior contributions.
