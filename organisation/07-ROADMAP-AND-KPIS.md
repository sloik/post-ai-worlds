# A roadmap governed by evidence

A roadmap is useful when it exposes what must be learned before the next commitment. It becomes misleading when an illustrative sequence acquires dates and revenue targets that readers mistake for observed progress. This public proposal uses gates, with no claim that Omnicorp has already crossed them.

## Four questions before expansion

First, can a bounded process produce a useful result under stated conditions? Second, does someone repeatedly want the result? Third, can the organisation deliver it at an acceptable total cost? Fourth, can it expand without losing the ability to inspect, correct and stop its work?

These questions should not be collapsed into one score. A strong demonstration can coexist with absent demand. Revenue can coexist with costly support. A successful first product can depend on knowledge that is difficult to transfer to another domain. Record the state of each gate, including unknowns.

The proposed sequence is to establish one useful capability, make it repeatable, add an adjacent engine and only then test broader expansion. Its timing depends on the task, market and operating environment. Returning to an earlier stage after a failure is a correction, not a reason to erase the earlier evidence.

## Measure outcomes and resources together

Revenue per person can help describe commercial leverage. It does not establish AI causation, profitability, welfare or responsibility. Pair it with accepted outcomes, total delivery cost, review effort, incidents and continuity. A ratio can improve because its denominator was cut while the organisation became less capable.

For each engine, record demand, renewal, cost to serve and the conditions under which output was accepted. For the organisation, examine whether a new authorised task can be started, completed and recovered from failure without reconstructing its process from memory.

For development itself, proposed measures include useful capability retained across tasks, regressions, reversion, adaptation cost and performance on previously unseen problems. These are measurement candidates. Until a workload and baseline exist, they are not established key performance indicators.

## Track learning rather than ceremony

A growing archive, test suite or agent count can indicate useful investment or accumulated overhead. Ask what a new artifact changes: did it prevent a recurrence, improve a decision or make a dependency visible? Removing an obsolete procedure can be an improvement too.

A review should compare results with a credible prior process and explain selection effects. Keep observed output rate separate from perceived speed and from an estimated no-AI counterfactual. Uncertainty in the comparison should remain visible.

The [shared worlds](../worlds/README.md) distinguish successful diffusion, dependence and plateau. This roadmap describes intended decisions within those conditions. It is neither a forecast that the organisation will grow nor permission to proceed merely because a milestone appears on a chart.
