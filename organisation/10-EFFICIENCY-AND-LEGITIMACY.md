# Efficiency and legitimacy are separate assessments

Efficiency asks what useful result an organisation obtains for the resources it consumes. Legitimacy asks why its decisions and effects are justified, and how affected people can challenge them. Verification can support both assessments, but neither follows automatically from the other.

## Count the whole process

Measure an accepted outcome alongside generation, review, correction, support and incident costs. State the workload and acceptance method. A cheap draft that requires extensive repair should not be compared with a finished result as though they were equivalent units.

Use separate denominators where necessary: money, energy, time and attention are not interchangeable without explicit conversion assumptions. Avoid counting the same expenditure twice. When an outcome cannot be valued credibly, report the observable quantities and the uncertainty rather than invent a precise return ratio.

Counterbalanced metrics help expose trade-offs, but do not make a system impossible to game. An organisation can improve several reported measures while changing the workload, excluding difficult cases or shifting costs onto others. Preserve a route for evidence that does not fit the dashboard.

## Keep acceptance distinct from approval

Customer satisfaction can be informative about a service. It does not authorise public rule-making or establish that everyone affected consented. A contract may give a supplier a bounded role while leaving noncustomers exposed to its decisions. A highly efficient operation can also produce unjustified harm.

NIST's AI risk framework identifies governance and assessment as continuing organisational functions. It is useful here because responsibility cannot be replaced by a single product score. This chapter proposes a distinction, not a claim that any framework certifies legitimacy. [NIST AI RMF](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework).

## Cooperation has conditions

Repeated interaction can make cooperation attractive when conduct is observable, future relationships matter and affected parties can impose credible consequences. Those conditions may fail. An indispensable supplier, hidden action, short horizon or coercive authority can sustain an extractive arrangement.

Omnicorp's proposed commitments to honest evidence, practical alternatives and effective remedies therefore need their own justification. They should not depend solely on a promise that markets eventually punish misconduct. Opposition, enforceable sanction and actual repair are different observations.

## A proposed steering loop

Observe outcomes and dependencies; assess them against explicit purposes and rights; make a bounded change; examine its consequences; retain or revise the resulting lesson. Include affected perspectives and failure reports rather than relying entirely on the organisation's own satisfaction measures.

The [worlds](../worlds/README.md) vary the institutions that make this loop effective. `OMNI-103` assumes meaningful public capacity; `OMNI-104` allows concentrated control. Neither should borrow the other's enforcement conditions without recording a change in premise.
