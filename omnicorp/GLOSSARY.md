# Organisational glossary

These definitions describe how this public edition uses its terms. They are not claims that the proposed organisation already implements every function. Related scenario identities live in the [shared worlds](../worlds/README.md).

**Accepted outcome.** A result evaluated against a named purpose and acceptance method under stated conditions. Acceptance does not imply unlimited correctness or remove the need for a remedy.

**Answerability.** The ability to identify a responsible operator, examine the basis of a decision and obtain meaningful correction or consequences. Operational tracing, moral responsibility and legal liability remain distinct questions.

**Authority.** A bounded entitlement to make a decision or take an action. A capability score, successful demonstration or commercial relationship does not automatically enlarge it.

**Capability.** What an actor or system can do under specified conditions. Access to capability differs from control of the resources needed to operate it.

**Continual organisational development.** The cornerstone: improving the organisation's ability to understand, learn and act while preserving the properties and commitments that make improvement useful.

**Evidence lineage.** The relationship between an observation, its primary record and later reports. Several accounts of one experiment do not constitute several independent experiments.

**Formal assurance.** A precisely scoped result about an encoded statement or model. Its identity includes relevant inputs and assumptions. It is distinct from empirical validation and implementation conformance.

**Governance.** The arrangements by which purposes, permissions, responsibilities, disputes and revisions are decided. A workflow is part of governance only insofar as it affects those arrangements.

**Independent capability.** Capability that can be usefully operated without a particular supplier's continuing discretion, within the stated dependencies and resource conditions. Downloadable weights alone do not establish this.

**Operating layer.** The records, permissions, task conventions, evaluation and integration arrangements that coordinate work. It can include both software and institutional practices.

**Organisational capital.** Useful accumulated knowledge, processes, relationships and tools. Its value must be demonstrated through future work; an archive's size is not a sufficient measure.

**Owned versus granted leverage.** A distinction between control backed by a usable claim or asset and access dependent on another party's continuing permission. Neither label removes the need to examine enforcement and physical dependencies.

**Product engine.** A repeatable operation directed at a recurring beneficiary need. It is a proposed unit of activity, not a guarantee of revenue or positive social effect.

**Public mandate.** Justification for making binding public decisions, involving representation, rights and meaningful challenge. It differs from competence, popularity and a service contract.

**Scenario branch.** A versioned set of scoped assumptions and claims used to examine a possible development. It is not necessarily a forecast, mutually exclusive future or complete social model.

**Verification.** Examination of whether an artifact or claim meets a specified criterion. The criterion, method and correspondence to the intended outcome remain open to review.
