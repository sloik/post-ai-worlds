# Resources that sustain development

Continual organisational development needs resources, but resource accumulation is not the same as development. An organisation can become richer while its understanding, adaptability or responsibility deteriorates. The proposed capital function asks what a commitment enables, what dependency it creates and what happens if the expected benefit does not arrive.

This chapter proposes general resource-allocation questions. It does not recommend financial products, trading strategies, tax arrangements or a funding decision for a particular person.

## Separate operating continuity from experiments

Distinguish resources needed to honour existing commitments from resources available for uncertain expansion. A useful experiment has a question, a bounded cost, an observation that would change a decision and a stopping condition. Calling spending an investment does not establish that it will produce an asset.

Include computation, energy, equipment, review, support and opportunity cost. A cheaper model can be more expensive per accepted outcome if it requires more attempts or correction. An owned machine can still depend on replacement parts, energy and specialist maintenance. Compare total costs under the workload that actually matters.

## Revenue as feedback

Paying customers can provide evidence that someone values an outcome. Revenue alone does not establish social benefit, durable demand or low delivery cost. A buyer may be testing a novelty, receiving a subsidy or unable to switch. Record renewal, complaints, redress and the alternatives available to the buyer.

Reinvestment should address a named bottleneck. More compute might help a constrained workload; it might also produce more unreviewed output. A distribution investment might reveal demand; it might simply make an unsuitable product more visible. The proposal is to compare observable results rather than assume a universal reinvestment multiplier.

## Funding changes governance

External funding can support research, infrastructure or expansion that internal revenue cannot support soon enough. It can also introduce control rights, reporting obligations and pressure to pursue a different horizon. Assess these terms alongside the amount received.

Ownership should be described precisely: ownership of a financial claim, an operating asset, data, a licence and a decision right are different. A return paid at another party's discretion may provide income without practical independence. Public-interest ownership likewise needs a mechanism for representation and accountability; changing the shareholder does not settle those questions.

The IMF discusses how AI could change income distribution and the fiscal policies used to broaden benefits. This supports examining who receives gains and bears transition costs, not predicting that any particular financing arrangement will work. [IMF analysis](https://www.imf.org/en/Publications/Staff-Discussion-Notes/Issues/2024/06/11/Broadening-the-Gains-from-Generative-AI-The-Role-of-Fiscal-Policies-549639).

In the [shared worlds](../worlds/README.md), useful capability may spread while productive assets remain concentrated. A resource plan should therefore explain both the organisation's agency and its effects on other people's ability to act.
