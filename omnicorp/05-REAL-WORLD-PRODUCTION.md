# Moving from digital artifacts to physical operations

An organisation that produces useful software has not thereby demonstrated that it can operate a clinic, a factory or a mine. Physical activity adds equipment, materials, maintenance, exposure to hazards and institutional responsibilities. Better reasoning can change a constraint without making every constraint disappear.

## Begin with a bounded intervention

A useful first question is whether existing operators have a decision or coordination problem that better software can help solve. Examples include preparing information for review, analysing equipment observations or planning a workflow. These are candidate applications, not claims of clinical or industrial validation.

Working with an incumbent can provide access to assets and domain knowledge. It can also make the new service dependent on that incumbent's capacity and willingness to act. If output is limited by an unavailable component or a lengthy physical process, supplying another recommendation may not increase production. The right question is which constraint the proposed intervention changes.

## Domain questions before a deployment claim

| Domain | A question the operation must answer |
|---|---|
| Software | Which behaviour is checked, and how are failures corrected? |
| Media | Are rights, provenance and the basis of audience trust clear? |
| Finance | Who bears risk, and what authority does the service actually hold? |
| Healthcare | What evidence supports the intended use, and who retains responsibility for care? |
| Steel and manufacturing | Which operating envelope, maintenance and equipment constraints govern the change? |
| Mining and extraction | How do site conditions, environmental effects and physical logistics affect the proposal? |

This is a comparison aid, not a universal ranking or a declaration that any sector is open for autonomous deployment.

## Treat constraints separately

Consider permission, capital, physical capacity, evidence and operational competence as different gates. A licence does not establish reliability. A successful laboratory demonstration does not grant permission to expose other people to its risks. Available money does not guarantee that a supplier can deliver the required equipment on the desired schedule.

Energy illustrates the distinction between digital capability and material expansion. The IEA analyses the electricity requirements of data centres and the infrastructure needed to supply them. Its scenarios are useful external inputs; they do not establish a particular facility's available power or an unlimited expansion path. [IEA, Energy and AI](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai).

## Build the evidence chain

For a bounded physical pilot, retain the operating envelope, calibration information, configuration, responsibility for interventions and observed deviations. Distinguish simulation from bench testing and operation in the intended environment. A result should be traceable to the conditions under which it was produced.

Testing against the physical world can expose errors that a software fixture misses. Measurement can still be incomplete, misinterpreted or manipulated. Independent observation, suitable instruments and incident response remain important. Physics constrains what happens; it does not automatically tell an organisation what happened or who should answer for it.

## Expansion remains conditional

Future AI could help design machines, improve fabrication or coordinate resource development. Those resources could support further capability. The strength and speed of that feedback are empirical questions. Research, construction and validation may impose delays; a useful next improvement may require more resources than the last one.

`OMNI-201` and `OMNI-202` separate material abundance from ownership. `OMNI-301` and `OMNI-401` distinguish continued improvement from a relevant plateau. The [shared worlds](../worlds/README.md) let us examine these conditions without treating physical expansion as a promised consequence of a software success.
