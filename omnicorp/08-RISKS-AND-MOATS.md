# Risks, dependence and defensibility

The main strategic risk is confusing access to a capable model with a durable organisation. A useful operation also depends on demand, reliable execution, resources, relationships and permission. Any of these can change while a model's benchmark performance improves.

## How an AI-native operation could fail

Generation can exceed review capacity. Parallel work can create incompatible changes. A repeated evaluation can become a target rather than an informative test. A provider can alter availability or terms. A product can attract attention without solving a problem people continue to value. These are candidate failure mechanisms; their importance must be assessed for a named operation.

Security exposure can grow faster than maintenance. OpenAI's November 2026 Linux kernel report says that, for the near and medium term, a low-privilege account on a system that is behind on patches should be treated as functionally equivalent to root for a well-resourced attacker. That is the producer's judgment, not our finding. [OpenAI, 2026](https://openai.com/index/a-thousand-fewer-paths-to-root/). If it holds, patch latency and isolation become operating requirements for an AI-native organisation, including its own agent hosts and model servers. A small operation with many agents and long-lived machines would bear this cost early.

Expansion adds another risk: assuming a success transfers across domains. A service that produces an inspectable draft and a machine acting near people have different failure costs. A convenient supplier relationship may conceal a dependency on scarce equipment or another organisation's willingness to cooperate.

Financial continuity also matters. An experiment that consumes operating resources can threaten existing obligations before its potential is resolved. A founder, specialist or undocumented process can become a single point of failure. The response is not to promise perfect redundancy, but to test absence, recovery and substitution under realistic conditions.

## What might remain valuable

A customer relationship, specialised judgment, useful operational data and a reliable process can create an advantage. None should be labelled permanently unfillable merely because it is difficult to copy today. Customers can move; preferences can change; legal protections can change; a new technique can reduce the value of an accumulated workflow.

Describe defensibility relative to an actor, a task and a horizon. A facility that takes years to reproduce may matter greatly over a shorter planning interval without being impossible to reproduce. A licence may protect an operation within one jurisdiction without creating universal control.

OECD's infrastructure analysis identifies concentration and entry barriers in inputs supporting AI. It motivates examining supplier dependencies rather than inferring independence from model access. It does not establish an inevitable monopoly. [OECD analysis](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/competition-in-artificial-intelligence-infrastructure_623d1874-en.html).

## Power is not the same as value

A firm can remain powerful because customers lack alternatives. Retention therefore cannot by itself establish consent or social benefit. Likewise, the organisation's ownership of its own tools can protect its agency without protecting anyone else's.

The proposal is to distinguish competitive advantage from justified authority and useful contribution. Answerability, interoperability and meaningful remedies are commitments worth evaluating even when extraction might be commercially sustainable.

`OMNI-101` and `OMNI-102` separate useful independent capability from dependent users. `OMNI-103` and `OMNI-104` separate accountable public capacity from concentrated control. The [worlds](../worlds/README.md) prevent one favourable condition from silently supplying the conclusion of another branch.
