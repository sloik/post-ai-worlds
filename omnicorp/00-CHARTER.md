# Omnicorp: purpose and method

Omnicorp is a research proposal for an organisation built around AI as an active participant in understanding and doing work. Its cornerstone is **continual development of the organisation's ability to understand, learn and act**. Products, revenue and physical operations sustain that development and expose it to consequences. They provide resources and feedback; they are not the whole purpose.

This is an ambition to investigate, not a declaration that an autonomous general-purpose company already exists. The central question is organisational: how could a small, accountable group assemble useful capabilities, preserve what it learns, and expand into unfamiliar domains without losing control of its commitments?

## The working hypothesis

A better model is not automatically a better institution. Useful deployment also requires changes to workflows, information, skills, incentives and responsibility. Research on the productivity J-curve models complementary intangible investment and the resulting difficulty of measuring productivity during a transition. It supports taking organisational change seriously; it does not predict Omnicorp's success or a fixed adoption timetable. [Brynjolfsson, Rock and Syverson](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25148).

Our design hypothesis is that an organisation can make those complements explicit from the beginning. A task should have a purpose, an authorised owner, a way to assess the result, and a record that helps the next task. Learning must improve future action rather than merely enlarge an archive.

## Commitments and uncertainties

The proposed organisation distinguishes the ability to act from permission to act. Technical superiority, commercial success and public authority are different things. A system might become highly capable while remaining dependent on suppliers, vulnerable to failure or unable to justify decisions affecting other people.

Current governance must identify who may authorise work and who carries responsibility. This does not assert that answerability is permanently restricted to humans. Future nonhuman responsibility would require more than memory or persuasive explanations: identity, recognised duties, consequences and effective remedies would need an institutional arrangement.

We also separate an outcome we want from a claim about what will happen. Cooperation, meaningful participation and practical freedom to leave are design commitments. They are not assumed to prevail automatically in markets or politics.

## How to read and challenge the proposal

The [blueprint](01-BLUEPRINT.md) describes a candidate architecture. The [self-development chapter](13-CONTINUAL-SELF-DEVELOPMENT.md) develops the cornerstone, including conditional model adaptation. The [shared worlds](../worlds/README.md) identify assumptions under stable `OMNI-*` numbers. A chapter's relevance to a world is not evidence that its conclusions hold there.

Contributions should make a proposition more useful to assess: supply primary evidence, expose a missing assumption, explain a counterexample, or propose a narrower experiment. An attractive story is a starting point. The project earns confidence through results and corrections that others can inspect.
